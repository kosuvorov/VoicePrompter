How do you use a teleprompter for TikTok?

For TikTok, keep the script in a narrow band next to the front camera, use short lines, and let voice tracking control the pace. The prompter should occupy as little of the screen as possible while remaining readable.

With VoicePrompter on a compatible mobile device, load the script, start the floating window, position it beside the lens, then open TikTok and record. The overlay is for you, not part of the camera image. If the current TikTok or operating-system version limits floating overlays, record in VoicePrompter's camera and upload the clip to TikTok afterward.

TikTok scripts benefit from aggressive formatting. Put the hook on its own line. Keep one spoken thought per line. Add blank lines where you want a beat. A wide paragraph forces your eyes to scan across the phone and makes the read visible.

How do you use a teleprompter for Instagram Reels?

The same floating-window setup works for Instagram Reels, but decide first whether you need Instagram's camera effects during recording. If you do, float the script over Instagram. If you do not, record clean vertical footage inside VoicePrompter and add music, captions, and effects during the Reel upload.

For face-to-camera Reels, place the text directly below the lens and keep the first lines around eye level. For demonstrations or product clips, shrink the prompt and move it closer to the action area so you can glance between the script and the object without looking lost.

The script itself should sound spoken. Replace formal transitions with the words you actually use. If you would never say a sentence to a friend, it will sound unnatural in a Reel even when the prompter setup is perfect.

How do you use a teleprompter for YouTube Shorts?

For YouTube Shorts, use the same vertical setup but write with slightly more room for explanation. A Short can support a clear hook, one useful idea, an example, and a close without feeling rushed.

Why voice scrolling matters more in short videos

Short-form delivery changes speed constantly. The hook may be fast, the explanation slower, and the final line punchy. A fixed scroll speed cannot anticipate those changes.

Word-tracking voice scrolling advances when you say the script and waits when you pause. VoicePrompter checks the spoken words against the whole script, so it can recover after an ad-lib, skipped sentence, or repeated line. That matters when one imperfect sentence would otherwise force you to restart a 30-second clip.

Sound-based scrolling is different. It reacts to noise or silence without knowing which words you said. It can pause when you stop talking, but it cannot find your place after you jump to another part of the script. The teleprompter scrolling comparison explains all five methods.

How do you avoid the obvious teleprompter stare?

Put the script closer to the lens, narrow the text column, increase the font size, and stop trying to read a whole paragraph at once.

The visible stare usually comes from horizontal scanning. A short line lets your eyes stay in one small area. Position the next sentence around camera height and let the completed text move away naturally. Looking directly at the words should look almost identical to looking at the lens.

Your pacing matters just as much. A fixed-speed prompter creates a regular cadence that sounds read. Voice scrolling lets you pause, change emphasis, and take a breath without losing the next line. The longer technique guide shows how to read a script without looking like you are reading.

What should a 30-second short-form script look like?

A 30-second script normally needs a hook, one idea, one example, and one close. Do not fill the full 30 seconds simply because the platform allows it.

Use this structure:

  1. Hook: one sentence that names the problem or surprising result.
  2. Context: one sentence explaining why it matters.
  3. Payoff: two or three short sentences delivering the useful idea.
  4. Close: one natural next step.

Format every spoken beat on a separate line. Read the draft aloud before recording. If you run out of breath or stumble twice, rewrite the line instead of practicing a sentence that was never meant to be spoken.

Should you record inside the social app or upload later?

Record inside TikTok or Instagram when you need their live camera effects, platform-specific controls, or a direct posting workflow. Record in a dedicated camera when image quality, repeatable settings, and clean source files matter more.

A floating prompter supports both approaches where the operating system and recording app allow it. In-app VoicePrompter recording is the predictable fallback. The prompter and camera are designed together, then the finished vertical video can be uploaded anywhere.
